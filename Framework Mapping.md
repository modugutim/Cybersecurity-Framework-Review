## Overview

In cybersecurity, framework mapping refers to the process of aligning or comparing different
cybersecurity frameworks, standards, or controls to one another. This helps organizations ensure
they are meeting various compliance requirements and managing security comprehensively.
Key Aspects of Framework Mapping:
1. Alignment of Controls:
o Maps security controls from one framework (e.g., NIST CSF) to equivalent
controls in another (e.g., ISO/IEC 27001).
o Helps identify overlaps, gaps, and redundancies.
2. Compliance Management:
o Essential for organizations that need to comply with multiple regulatory
requirements (e.g., HIPAA, GDPR, PCI-DSS).
o Allows for consolidated reporting and audit preparation.
3. Risk Management:
o Facilitates understanding how different frameworks address specific risks.
o Enhances decision-making in adopting and prioritizing controls.
4. Efficiency and Consistency:
o Enables organizations to reuse existing control implementations across
frameworks.
o Supports unified security policy development and maintenance.

Example Use Case:
An organization using the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) may map its controls to
ISO/IEC 27001 and CIS Controls to ensure comprehensive coverage and make audits more
efficient.
